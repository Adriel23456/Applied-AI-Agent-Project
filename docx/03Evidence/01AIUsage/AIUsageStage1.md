## 2. Where AI was used and what was done manually afterwards

### 2.1 Topic delimitation and framing

**What the tool did.** Claude was used in an extensive exploratory conversation to examine possible project directions before the team settled on this one. That conversation initially explored a reinforcement learning direction (coverage path planning in unknown terrain) which was **abandoned**; the current Track A topic replaced it after the team's own reevaluation and the professor's feedback.
The tool contributed to: refining the research question to make it falsifiable, distinguishing the three required review axes of the course, and testing whether the proposed differentiators against previous works were technically defensible or merely rhetorical.

**Manually verified.** The team directly read the professor's feedback and made the topic decision on their own. Every framing statement that survived in the proposal was contrasted against the course prompt and primary sources.

**Not generated by AI.** The choice of topic, the choice of track, the research question as ultimately formulated, and the decision to abandon the RL direction.

### 2.2 Literature discovery

**What the tools did.** Consensus was used to identify keywords, build search strings, and find candidate peer-reviewed works according to the topic. Claude and ChatGPT were used to evaluate whether candidate articles actually supported the three axes, to mark which candidates were *preprints* instead of peer-reviewed publications, to identify redundancies within the candidate set, and to provide an initial classification of references.

**Manually verified.** The DOI of each reference must be resolved by a team member and its content read before it enters `refs.bib`. Each member's `.bib` files carry an explicit `VERIFIED` / `UNVERIFIED` status comment on each entry; **an entry not marked as VERIFIED does not appear in the document.** As of this writing, 2 out of 21 entries are verified and 19 are pending. AI interpretations of concepts were consistently contrasted with the original content of the sources.

**Not generated by AI.** The final selection of the 21 articles, the assignment of axes, the distribution of reading among team members, and the critical evaluation of their relevance were produced by the team.

**Explicitly not delegated.** No DOI, author list, publication venue, or page range was accepted based on a tool's assertion. When metadata could not be confirmed against a primary source, the field was left as a placeholder rather than filled with a plausible guess.

### 2.3 Dataset evaluation

**What the tool did.** Claude was used to discuss the implications of the dataset structure (many correlated snapshots per episode, distribution drift over days, coexistence of engine versions) and to reason about which of these constitute threats to validity.

**Manually verified.** All quantitative statements about the dataset come from the team's own audit scripts, not from any tool. Figures such as episode count, snapshot count, field completeness rate, and contamination measured under a random split are reproducible from `reports/audit_summary.md` and `reports/deep_probe.json` with a fixed seed.

**Not generated by AI.** The audit pipeline, its execution, and every number it produced.

### 2.4 Documentation and article structure

**What the tool did.** Claude and ChatGPT were used to draft and review the project documentation: the topic proposal, reading assignment documents, repository conventions, and the interpretation of how Stage 1 requirements (three axes, comparative table, gap identification, literature baseline, methodology) map into IEEE format sections. ChatGPT was also used to design a systematic analysis template for the articles, organize ideas, and adapt the vocabulary to a professional and technical tone. The AI was continuously asked to correct or adjust points and arguments to properly develop the document setup.

**Manually verified.** Each technical statement in those documents was verified against the course prompt or a primary source. Passages that could not be substantiated were removed rather than softened. The analysis templates were manually completed by the team members. All AI adjustments to arguments were manually reviewed to ensure they correctly reflected the team's viewpoints.

**Not generated by AI.** The formalization of the problem, the basic general idea and structure of how the entire document had to be drafted, the feature dictionary, the leakage blacklist, the experimental protocol, the baseline ladder, and every result that will be reported.

## 3. Limits established by the team

These rules are maintained regardless of which tool any member uses.

* **No reference enters the bibliography by blind recommendation of a tool.** Each DOI is manually resolved and the source is read to confirm it says what we attribute to it.
* **No technical decision is delegated.** The research question, objective definition, split design, choice of metrics, and interpretation of results are the team's own reasoning. A tool may suggest options; the team chooses and defends.
* **No experimental result is generated.** Every number reported in the article comes from code we wrote and executed.
* **Generated code is executed before being committed.** Nothing is merged assuming it works.
* **Preprints are marked as such.** When a tool suggested an arXiv-only work, it was excluded from the 21 peer-reviewed articles or consulted with the professor before inclusion.
